Mark candidates

Eight marks

Two marks each for the four best-available names. Every mark is drawn once on a 24-unit grid and shown at 120, 40, 22 and 16px on #0E0E0E — one ink, one accent, flat fills, sharp corners, no gradients and no soft shadows.

16px is the only test that counts, so it is the size to look at first. Marks that resolved into a letterform at 16px, or whose accent disappeared, were cut and redrawn rather than kept for the large view.

Flintbell

01 · Flintbell

Strike

Two masses driven together, with the accent as the contact between them. The excitation event all six engines begin with — something hits something, and what happens next is the sound. Drawn as converging blocks rather than one bar crossing another, because a crossed bar read as a dagger once it got small.

Weakest of the eight, and worth saying so: at 16px the two facing masses still read as a bowtie before they read as a strike. The blocks were made unequal to fight that and it only half works. Keep it for the 40px view, not for the tab icon.

02 · Flintbell

Fissure

A solid mass opening along a fault, with one chip shaken loose in the accent. The attack transient — the instant a struck material gives — which is the part of a sound the ear identifies an instrument by. Almost all of the mark is negative space, so it holds its shape as it shrinks.

Gravelbell

03 · Gravelbell

Aggregate

Four chips of unequal size and angle: sound handled as loose material instead of a continuous line. That is granular synthesis stated literally, and it is also what the name says. Solid fills rather than strokes, which is why it is still four distinct pieces at 16px.

04 · Gravelbell

Toll

Two walls of a resonator, held open so the form never closes into a circle, and the accent mass that drives them. Excitation inside a body is the whole of physical modelling, and it is the mechanism the other five engines are all doing a version of. The most literal mark here, and the most survivable when small.

Anviltine

05 · Anviltine

Anvil & Tine

A heavy body and the two partials it throws off, at unequal length because a harmonic series decays. The mass is the ground you strike; the tines are the only part you hear. Both tines are truncated rather than pointed — needle tips disappeared entirely at 16px.

06 · Anviltine

Comb

Two streams of tines entering from opposite edges and overlapping in the middle: material laid against itself, offset in time. It is what granular does with grains and what wavetable does with adjacent frames, and it is the reason both engines can sound like one moving body rather than a stack of copies.

Frostlattice

07 · Frostlattice

Window

A fixed body of material, and the single position currently being read. That one accent line is the wavetable scan position, the granular playhead and the sample start point — the same idea in three engines, and the control that makes a static table sound alive. The slab is tilted off-axis so its silhouette is never a rectangle.

08 · Frostlattice

Notch

A slab with a wedge cut out of it, and the removed wedge set down beside it. Subtractive synthesis said plainly: you arrive at the sound by taking material away, and this is the only mark here that shows you what left. The bite runs past centre and the edges slope, so the mass reads as notched rather than as a square with a dent.
